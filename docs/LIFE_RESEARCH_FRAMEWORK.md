## 1. The center of gravity

The long arc of the work is the **evolutionary and health histories of African
Americans in the United States, from the 17th century to the present** — and using
genomics to make those histories concrete, both biologically and historically.

Two intertwined aims sit at the top:

1. **Mechanism.** Understand how environmental stressors — including the structural
   forces of enslavement, forced migration, segregation, and ongoing racism — have
   shaped the **genomic and epigenomic landscape** of African-descended people in the
   U.S., and through it their phenotypes, fitness, and health.

2. **Restoration & witness.** Use that understanding to **connect living people to the
   ancestries severed by the transatlantic slave trade**, and to give a concrete
   biological and historical account of the **impact of white supremacy on
   populations** — turning a diffuse, often-denied history into measurable,
   documentable structure.

These are the questions that must not get lost. Everything below is in service of being
able to ask them rigorously.

## 2. The layered model that makes the apex questions askable

The reason the foundational pigmentation work matters is that the apex questions all
depend on a solid, layered understanding of how biology is organized and how
environment reaches it:

```
        evolutionary landscape  (what variation is possible — constraint, pleiotropy)
                 │
              genome             (sequence variation; the record of demography + selection)
                 │
            epigenome            (the regulatory layer where environment writes onto biology)
                 │
             phenome             (traits, physiology, health)
                 │
             fitness             (how environments select, over time)
```

You can't credibly claim "stressor X shaped the biology of population Y" without each
layer: a model of what was **evolvable** (landscape/constraint), a way to read **history
off the genome** (demography, selection, ancestry), a rigorous account of how
**environment acts through the epigenome**, and **valid phenotype/health measures** in
the populations of interest. The current program builds exactly these layers — on
pigmentation, where the biology is tractable and the data exist — so they're load-tested
before being pointed at the harder, higher-stakes diaspora questions.

## 3. Question hierarchy (so nothing gets buried)

**Apex questions** *(the life's work)*

- How did the transatlantic slave trade and the structures of white supremacy
  (17th c → present) shape the genome, epigenome, phenome, and health of African
  Americans?
- Can we reconnect living individuals to specific African source ancestries lost to
  that history?
- Can we make the biological and historical toll of these systems concrete and
  documentable, rather than abstract?

**Bridge questions** *(what turns the foundation into the apex — mostly future work)*

- Source-population reconstruction: which African (and other) ancestries contributed,
  at what proportions, with what regional specificity, to which individuals?
- Admixture dynamics and timing tied to the historical record (forced migration,
  domestic slave trade, post-emancipation movement).
- Selection and drift in the diaspora: where has the new environment (including
  structural stress and disease) left signatures on allele frequencies?
- Social/structural stress and the epigenome: what regulatory and health signatures
  does chronic structural racism leave, within a lifetime — and (carefully) across
  generations?

**Foundational questions** *(the current program — see strategy doc)*

- What constrains the pigmentation landscape (pleiotropy, network, LoF intolerance)? → P3
- How has selection shaped pigmentation in understudied populations, and what do
  haplotype/ancestry methods reveal? → P2
- How well do current genetic tools predict phenotype in underrepresented cohorts, and
  where do measurement and representation break down? → P1 + vitamin D / erythema

## 4. How each current project is a deliberate scaffold

Framed so the connection to the apex is explicit — none of these is a detour.

| Current work | Capability it builds | What the apex work will use it for |
|---|---|---|
| **Paper 2** — Melanesian selection/introgression: demography scaffold, ChromoPainter, local ancestry, IBD/haplotype methods, admixture inference | The entire **ancestry-reconstruction toolkit** + a methods proving ground on a clean case | **Connecting living people to lost African source ancestries**; dating admixture against the historical record |
| **Paper 1** — SAPPHIRE PRS: African-ancestry imputation panels, frequency-filtering, genotype→phenotype prediction in admixed cohorts | African-aware **prediction + representation methods** | Phenotype/health prediction in African American cohorts; honest handling of representation gaps |
| **Paper 3** — constraints & pleiotropy | The **evolutionary-landscape layer**: what's evolvable vs constrained | Interpreting diaspora selection signals; reasoning about which traits/loci respond to environment |
| **Vitamin D / erythema** — measurement validity in darker skin | A **valid phenome** layer | Any health-history claim depends on measures that work in the populations studied |
| **Methylation thread** (in P1) + future All of Us | First piece of the **epigenome / stress** layer | The mechanism by which structural stress reaches biology |

Read together: P2 builds the *ancestry engine*, P1 builds the *prediction/representation engine*, P3 supplies the *interpretive frame*, the measurement work guarantees the *phenotype is real*, and the epigenome thread opens the *environment→biology mechanism*. The diaspora program is what you get when these are aimed at African American cohorts and joined to the historical record.

## 5. What the apex thrust will additionally require (named now, so it's on the map)

- **Cohorts & references:** African American genomic/health cohorts (e.g., All of Us,
  and population studies of African-ancestry health) **+** dense, regionally resolved
  African source-population references (the same African panels Paper 1 assembles are
  the seed).
- **Methods to add to the toolkit:** local-ancestry deconvolution, IBD-to-source
  matching, admixture dating, and adaptive-admixture / ancestry-deviation selection
  scans — i.e., the ancestry engine from Paper 2 specialized for a recently admixed,
  historically documented population.
- **Epigenome / social-stress layer:** DNA-methylation and expression measures of
  chronic and structural stress (the "weathering" framework), built with care for
  confounding by ancestry and cell composition.
- **Historical integration:** linking genetic source signals to the documented geography
  and timeline of the slave trade and domestic forced migration; ancient/historical DNA
  only where community-led and ethically grounded.

## 6. Rigor and ethics as load-bearing, not garnish

This is the part that protects the work and the people in it.

- **Precision about mechanism.** Distinguish how these forces shaped the genome (via
  demography and selection across generations) from how they shape health now (via
  environment and the epigenome within lifetimes). Treat **transgenerational epigenetic
  inheritance in humans as contested** — the headline "trauma in the DNA" literature
  has largely not replicated, and confounding (ancestry, cell type, SES) is severe.
  Building the layered model carefully is exactly what lets these claims be made
  defensibly rather than dismissed.
- **Don't re-essentialize race.** Ancestry is genetic and continuous; race is a social
  construct. The work documents the biological *consequences of a social system*, which
  is the opposite of biologizing race — and the framing has to stay that sharp.
- **Community-engaged and restorative.** Reconnecting people to lost ancestry is
  personal and identity-bearing. Consent, data governance, and descendant-community
  partnership are first-order design constraints, consistent with your stated commitment
  to "generating data alongside communities... not confirming a predetermined story."

---

## 7. The throughline in one sentence

Build a rigorous, layered understanding of how the genome, epigenome, phenome, and
fitness landscape fit together — using tractable pigmentation biology to forge the
tools and the frame — and then turn that apparatus on the evolutionary and health
history of African Americans, to reconnect people to severed ancestries and make the
biological impact of white supremacy concrete, documentable, and undeniable.
