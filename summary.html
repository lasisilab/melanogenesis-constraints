1 · Core thesis & data

The poster argues that constraint and adaptation operate on partly orthogonal evolutionary axes in the melanogenesis network. Two strands:

  • Constraint strand: tissue-expression breadth and network connectivity each independently predict loss-of-function intolerance (LOEUF).
  • Adaptation strand: population-specific selection (PBS) targets different tissue-specificity regimes in African vs. Melanesian lineages.

2 · Session work — what changed

Multiple regression (reviewer-driven) — now 5-panel figure

A collaborator challenged the claim that connectivity and breadth independently affect constraint. Built a joint OLS in analysis/phase2_tau_variants.py and a figure in analysis/phase2_joint_regression_figure.py.

Result — claim holds. LOEUF ~ breadth + log1p(KEGG) (z-scored, n=127): βbreadth=−0.135 (p=1.3×10-3), βKEGG=−0.131 (p=1.8×10-3), VIF≈1.16. Using τ instead of breadth: βτ=+0.126 (p=3.6×10-3). Betweenness drops out once KEGG is included. Direction of τ: higher tissue specificity → higher LOEUF (less constrained) — expected, because broadly-expressed genes face stronger pleiotropic purifying selection.

Figure now has 5 panels (12.91" × 2.67", poster-ready): (A) Breadth | KEGG AV plot; (B) KEGG | breadth AV plot; (C) τ | KEGG AV plot; (D) KEGG | τ AV plot; (E) forest plot of standardized β ± 95% CI across all four bivariate specifications. → output/figure_phase2_joint_regression.{png,pdf}

GTEx expression heatmaps

Reworked the heatmap from raw log2(TPM+1) to row-normalized "τ-style" expression (each cell = x / row-max; τ = mean(1−this)). Removed the LOEUF side-bar. Explored several gene orderings and built a family of figures:

  • figure_phase1_gtex_heatmap_limited — 43 curated tissues, rows hierarchically clustered by expression profile, tissues grouped by biological system.
  • figure_phase1_gtex_heatmap_skin_sorted — 3 panels: Welch's t (skin vs non-skin), τ-split, and composite (t×τ).
  • figure_phase1_gtex_heatmap_skin_split — standalone τ-split: tissue-specific (τ≥0.7) block on top, broadly-expressed below, rows sorted by skin Welch's t within each block.
  • figure_phase1_gtex_heatmap_skin_split_selection — the τ-split with a PBS-quadrant side-bar overlaying the selection axis onto the expression heatmap (see below).

Adaptation × tissue specificity (poster section fix)

A reviewer couldn't see how the PBS quadrant scatter demonstrated "Adaptation exploits tissue specificity, not constraint." Built a focused 2-panel companion (figure_phase2_adaptation_tau_contrast) showing τ and LOEUF violins for African-specific vs Melanesian-specific quadrants only.

The demonstration. Among population-specifically-selected network genes: τ differs between African- and Melanesian-targeted genes (Mann-Whitney p=0.032) while LOEUF does not (p=0.97). African selection → broadly expressed genes; Melanesian selection → tissue-restricted genes.

τ-split × selection overlay

The two axes connect. Overlaying PBS quadrant onto the τ-split heatmap and testing the contingency: of the population-specifically-selected genes in the tissue-specific block, 7/8 are Melanesian-targeted; the broadly-expressed block splits 21 African vs 15 Melanesian. Fisher's exact OR=0.10, p=0.046. The selection axis aligns with the tissue-specificity axis as predicted.

Within-network PBS association — the primary selection finding

Among the 129 network genes, does tissue specificity or breadth predict population-specific PBS? Two datasets tested (both in analysis/phase2_pbs_within_network{,_combined}.py; compact row figures via phase2_pbs_within_network_smallfigs.py):

Combined dataset (HGDP+1KGP+SGDP) — significant signal: within-network PBS percentile (0–100 rank among the 129 genes) regressed on z-scored predictors + covariates (log1p SNP count, log10 body length); 10,000-shuffle permutation p.
  • AFR τ: adjβ=−6.89, perm p=0.011* — broadly-expressed genes have higher African PBS (n≈127)
  • AFR breadth: adjβ=+7.88, perm p=0.003** — confirming direction; consistent sign, independent predictors
  • MEL τ: adjβ=+5.28, perm p=0.048* — tissue-specific genes have higher Melanesian PBS
  • MEL breadth: adjβ=−6.18, perm p=0.021*
  • EUR/SAS: all null — lineage-specificity control holds
  • KEGG, betweenness, LOEUF: ns in all branches
The AFR and MEL τ signals go in opposite directions — this is the quantitative version of the "Adaptation exploits tissue specificity" story. → output/figure_phase2_pbs_within_network_combined_row.{png,pdf} (poster figure 1)
Dataset-robustness caveat: The SGDP-only genome-wide percentile version of this analysis is null (all permutation p > 0.13; AFR τ Spearman ρ=+0.075 SGDP vs ρ=−0.234 combined — a sign flip). This is purely a dataset effect (African n≈18 in SGDP vs 747 in combined), not a percentile-transform artifact (Spearman is rank-invariant). The combined signal is better-powered but is within-network only (no genome-wide background). Report as dataset-dependent; present combined as primary with the SGDP null as an honest caveat. → output/figure_phase2_pbs_within_network_row.{png,pdf}

4 · Key new finding — network enrichment (a careful negative)

The melanogenesis network is NOT significantly enriched for elevated PBS vs. the genome-wide background (SGDP).
  • PBS-1 African: network median 0.302 vs background 0.293; MWU p=0.15; SNP-matched perm p=0.24.
  • PBS-3 Melanesian: network median 0.330 vs background 0.311; MWU p=0.18; perm p=0.25.
  • Network genes in genome-wide top 1%: 0 of 123 (expected ~1.2). Top 5%: 5-6 (expected ~6.2) — at chance.

Two things make this interpretable rather than disappointing:

  • The high genome-wide baseline (~0.31) is real, not an artifact. It holds for genes with 100+ SNPs and n_snps barely correlates with PBS (ρ=0.07). It reflects deep divergence of the SGDP populations — PBS is a relative/outlier statistic, so the median is demographic baseline; the tail is what matters.
  • The poster claim was never network-level enrichment. It's the within-network distribution of selection (African→broad, Melanesian→specific), which holds independently (Fisher p=0.046).
Individual genes still rank high genome-wide (PBS-3 percentiles): KITLG 98.6, ATF2 98.6, HIF1A 97.7, PRKACA 96.9, BAK1 95.9, CREB1 95.8; MITF 85.8, TYRP1 86.6. KITLG is a known pigmentation selection target — a nice positive control. Per-gene percentiles saved to output/network_gene_genomewide_percentiles_SGDP.csv.

Suggested honest framing: "Individual pigmentation effectors (KITLG, ATF2, HIF1A) rank in the genome-wide top 2-3% for population-specific PBS, though the network as a whole is not enriched above genomic background (MWU p=0.18) — consistent with selection acting on specific effectors rather than the entire pathway."

6 · Open questions for ideation

A. Dataset robustness — how to frame the SGDP null honestly on the poster?

The combined-dataset result drives the story, but the SGDP-only genome-wide version is null. Best framing: footnote or caption noting "combined dataset (HGDP+1KGP+SGDP), within-network percentile; SGDP-only genome-wide version is null at current sample sizes (African n=18) — see supplementary." The direction and effect size in the combined data is the primary claim.

B. Is the "orthogonal axes" story now complete?

The three figures jointly tell: (1) breadth and KEGG each independently predict LOEUF (joint regression, 5-panel); (2) tissue specificity splits the expression architecture (heatmap); (3) selection on tissue specificity is lineage-specific and goes in opposite directions in African vs. Melanesian (within-network PBS row figure). The quadrant Fisher test (p=0.046) and Mann-Whitney τ contrast (p=0.032) are strong supporting stats for the caption.

C. Does LOEUF need controlling in the within-network PBS model?

The earlier raw-PBS regression showed AFR τ survives LOEUF control (τ p=0.022 with LOEUF in the model). This hasn't been repeated for the within-network percentile outcome — could be a confirmatory analysis worth running if a reviewer presses on whether τ is proxying constraint.
